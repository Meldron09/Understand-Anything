# Diagnostic ladder

Four short-answer rungs on the learner's own topic words (never a source, never multiple choice). Plain chat, one rung at a time, easiest first. Offer "skip" once before rung 1.

| Rung | Template (fill from the topic) | Probes |
|---|---|---|
| `define` | "In your own words, what is <topic>?" | vocabulary |
| `why` | "Why does <topic> work this way, or what problem does it solve?" | the reason, not the formula |
| `apply` | "Here is a small case: <one-line scenario>. How would you use <topic> on it?" | use on a new case |
| `edge` | "When does <topic> break down or behave surprisingly?" | limits |

## Grading

Grade each answer at once, silently, as one of:

- `solid`: correct, in the learner's words, with the reason where the rung asks for it.
- `shaky`: partly right, or right with a gap or misconception.
- `missed`: wrong, blank, or "no idea".

Do not say the grade or teach the answer; a one-word acknowledgement, then the next rung. **Stop at the first `missed`**; rungs not asked are omitted from the evidence. A `shaky` does not stop the ladder.

## Level

Count the `solid` rungs: 0-1 `novice`, 2-3 `intermediate`, 4 `advanced`.

Skipped ladder: `level: novice`, `assumed: [level]`, evidence line `none (ladder skipped; level assumed)`.

## Self-rating

Ask after the ladder (also after a skip): "Which fits you best on <topic>: novice, intermediate or advanced?" Record it as the last evidence line, `self-rating: <their answer> (ladder says <level>)`, or `(ladder skipped)`. It is for comparison only and never sets `level`.

## Evidence lines

One per asked rung, in `brief.md` under `## Level evidence`: `<rung> | <question as asked> | <grade> | <few words why>`, rung being `define`, `why`, `apply` or `edge`. Name the core concepts a question touches in the question text so the lesson can match on them.

## Core concepts

After the ladder, propose 3-5 noun phrases from the goal and the `shaky` or `missed` rungs (general knowledge, not sources). The learner confirms or edits; keep their wording.
